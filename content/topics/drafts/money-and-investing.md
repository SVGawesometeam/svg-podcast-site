## Marina's take

How do I manage, grow and diversify my money? The guests on this hub do not agree on the portfolio.

Humphrey Yang, a former financial advisor, starts with saving 20% of income when the average American saves about 4%, then a three to six month emergency fund in a high-yield account, then a three-fund portfolio: roughly 50% US stocks, 25 to 30% international, the rest in bonds. Crypto capped near 5%. Alex, the former rocket scientist who has paid for his life from investing for ten years, holds no bonds at all, has 40% of his portfolio in Nvidia and about 25% in Palantir, and says behaviour drives about 90% of returns. Both say the same thing about downturns: do not sell.

My own habits are in the solo episodes. I pay myself a fixed salary, track every income source in a monthly spreadsheet, cut what does not earn, and route money to taxes, savings and investments the moment a payment lands. Small spending, the $6 coffee and $18 takeout, added up to over $11,000 a year when I checked. I have 19 income streams and earn over $100,000 a month across them; investment income is now close to business income. The Hawaii condo brought $50,000 of Airbnb revenue in six months, and one surprise bill still put it in the red.

None of this is advice. It is what each person does with their own money, with the numbers attached, so you can compare.

## Questions people ask

### Where do I start if I have nothing invested?

Humphrey Yang (/episode/pUITF0uGa6U/) says save until you hold three to six months of expenses, $6,000 to $12,000 if you spend $2,000 a month, in a high-yield account, and invest what comes in above that. In my 20s episode (/episode/QvwI0TTVcwo/) I say start with $20; my regret is the years I spent early income on luxury goods instead. Alex (/episode/S9xrvZhMLMI/) suggests automating a fixed amount a month into the S&P 500 and NASDAQ 100 if you never want to think about the market again.

### Should I buy a house or keep renting?

Humphrey Yang (/episode/pUITF0uGa6U/) rents in San Francisco because the same money as a mortgage would get him a one-bedroom condo instead of his two-bedroom, and he would only buy to live in, since he does not want to be a landlord. I bought a condo on Hawaii's Big Island in January 2024 (/episode/JqcK4b3wRGU/) because occupancy there runs 85 to 90% or more and new short-term rental permits were still available; the property manager takes 20%.

### How many income streams do I need?

I have 19 (/episode/d_Dp1vpDCrk/), and one of them, LinguaTrip, currently pays me $0 in distributions while we grow it toward a sale. Digital products bring over $10,000 a month, with about 21% of that from email to existing customers. In the 13 habits episode (/episode/qrzRg0JUBj0/) I explain how online courses kept LinguaTrip alive through COVID when in-person bookings collapsed. Ankur Jain (/episode/PmpaFWBFy_c/) built Bilt on money people already spend: rent earns points worth about 2.2 cents each that transfer to airlines and hotels or go toward a down payment.

### Are AI stocks a bubble?

Alex (/episode/S9xrvZhMLMI/) says the hardware and infrastructure layer is not: Nvidia's revenue and profit followed its valuation, and he judges growth companies on forward PE one to three years out, treating under 30 as a benchmark. Roman Yampolskiy (/episode/00RHph_eok4/) takes a different frame: if cognitive labour costs $20 a month in two to three years, accumulating assets now is the last window. Luana Lopes Lara (/episode/xiJsfvaxzRY/) reads Kalshi's markets, where 73% of trades in the monthly tech-layoff market name AI as the top reason for cuts.

### What do I do when the market drops?

Nothing, according to both guests. Humphrey Yang (/episode/pUITF0uGa6U/) says selling in a downturn locks in the loss; his ideal holding period is forever. Alex (/episode/S9xrvZhMLMI/) puts about 90% of returns down to behaviour and uses the Fear and Greed Index to be greedy when others are fearful. My rule in the habits episode (/episode/xuN4jZpaPho/) is automation, so the decision never reaches me on a bad day.
