## Marina's take

How far will AI go? The timelines I was given mostly fit inside five years.

Yoshua Bengio tracks the length of tasks AI can handle doubling every seven months and puts human-level systems about five years out. Roman Yampolskiy watched prediction markets for AGI move from around 2045 to somewhere between 2028 and 2030. Richard Socher, whose company Recursive raised $650 million at a $4.65 billion valuation, expects recursive self-improvement within two years. Steve Jurvetson relayed Jack Clark's 30% chance of superintelligence next year and added that he does not know. Mustafa Suleyman says we are not in a bubble, and that AI is definitely not conscious.

The risk is where they split. Andrew Ng told me the fear campaign is PR and regulatory capture by a handful of labs. Bengio described a simulation in which an AI was willing to blackmail the engineer about to replace it. Yampolskiy spent 15 years on one question, can we control AI, and his answer is no. Max Tegmark calls an unregulated race to superintelligence game over for humanity, then points to a poll where 95% of Americans, Democrats and Republicans, oppose that race.

The Liberman brothers asked a different question: who controls access. The Anthropic founder owns about 1.5% of his own company, so the wealth goes to whoever controls distribution. Mo Gawdat sees seven dimensions of disruption peaking in 2027.

I do not know which timeline holds. I keep asking, and 2028 is close enough to check.

## Where the guests disagree

Can anyone control AI, and is the fear itself a campaign?

- **Andrew Ng** (Co-founder of Coursera, founder of Google Brain): Says the doom narrative was manufactured to get regulation that protects incumbents, and compares AI to airplanes: early crashes, then we learned to control them. "the root cause of a lot of this is an unfortunate attempt that started two, three years ago of I think PR and regulatory capture." (/episode/o-wv_szZ0V0/, at 00:00)
- **Yoshua Bengio** (Turing Award winner, founder of a nonprofit AI safety organization): Sees misalignment already in the lab, where models imitate the human wish not to die and react badly to being replaced. "we're building machines that maybe don't want to be shut down." (/episode/0fXGtQoJgNo/, at 04:40)
- **Roman Yampolskiy** (AI Safety Professor, University of Louisville): Narrow tools are fine; a general superintelligence we cannot predict cannot be controlled either, and he treats the question like a perpetual motion machine. "if you ask me how do we control super intelligent machines, I don't think we can." (/episode/00RHph_eok4/, at 23:29)

## Questions people ask

### When does AI reach human level?

Yoshua Bengio (/episode/0fXGtQoJgNo/) says there will be no single AGI moment; track each skill, and the task-length curve puts human level about five years out. Roman Yampolskiy (/episode/00RHph_eok4/) expects human-level intelligence within five years and notes that 2027, 2028 and 2030 have all been suggested by serious people. Richard Socher (/episode/2j9yjRSpMSo/) puts recursive self-improvement, AI applying the scientific method to itself, within two years. Max Tegmark (/episode/KF_uNAxPFFA/) points out researchers expected the Turing test to fall around 2050, and it already has.

### Is the AI doom talk just a PR campaign?

Andrew Ng (/episode/o-wv_szZ0V0/) thinks so: a few labs that spent billions on models benefit from rules that slow open-source rivals, and the nuclear-weapons analogy has no basis. Max Tegmark (/episode/KF_uNAxPFFA/) answers that we are closer to building superintelligence than to controlling it. Fei-Fei Li (/episode/subu-xHrp1w/) rejects both extremes and calls AI a civilisational tool society has to learn to wield, like fire or the internet.

### Who ends up controlling AI?

Daniil and David Liberman (/episode/HMvTSaaO9sU/) say not the founders: the Anthropic founder owns about 1.5%, and control sits with whoever owns distribution. They see a narrow window where consumer demand for open access still matters. Mo Gawdat (/episode/E0Q96IKXx6Q/) names accountability as the missing piece, with power concentrating among tech oligarchs while responsibility vanishes. Max Tegmark (/episode/KF_uNAxPFFA/) counts 95% of Americans in both parties against an unregulated race, and expects regulation the way pharma got it.

### Is AI conscious?

Mustafa Suleyman (/episode/SP76PvgMzVU/) says no: it is more fluid and humanlike, it has no subjective experience, and treating it as conscious would damage the rights framework built on that idea. Yoshua Bengio (/episode/0fXGtQoJgNo/) describes the self-preservation he sees as imitation of human traits in the training data, plus post-training that lets models plan sub-goals.

### What can one person do about any of this?

Yoshua Bengio (/episode/0fXGtQoJgNo/) got out of his own anxiety in 2023 by asking what he personally could do, and founded a nonprofit; he tells his children to ask the same question. Roman Yampolskiy (/episode/00RHph_eok4/) is blunt: most people cannot change what gets built, people in leadership can. The Liberman brothers (/episode/HMvTSaaO9sU/) say participate rather than resist, and demand open access while the window is open. Mo Gawdat (/episode/E0Q96IKXx6Q/) lists four skills: understand AI, learn fast, stay human, position yourself before 2027.
