## Marina's take

How do I start a business with AI, without funding or a technical team? I asked more than a dozen founders this year, and the answers line up.

Start with a problem you already know. Anton Osika says the best new founders are knowledge workers with 11 or more years in a job, because they understand the problem better than any developer. Amjad Masad told me about a CFO at a VC firm who built his own fund-management app on Replit in three months and is on track for $5 million in revenue. The numbers behind this are in the boring-businesses compilation: 76% of small businesses use AI, and 14% have built it into their operations.

Then get paid fast. Alex Mashrabov's playbook is two people, one builder and one go-to-market person, first dollar by day 30 and $1 million ARR by day 90. Anton's rule is talk to 10 people before you build, and never make the first product free. Grant Lee says friends will lie about your product. A credit card does not.

Funding is optional now. Alex raised $16 million for Higgsfield with no revenue and says he would not build that way today. Grant did over 100 pitches in two weeks and heard no more than half the time.

My own version is in the one-person business guide: pick an industry you know, find three repetitive tasks, and test a simple GPT-powered fix with five to ten real users a week.

## Where the guests disagree

Launch early and charge, or build in private until the product is clearly better?

- **Chris Pedregal** (CEO and Co-founder, Granola): Spent the first year in closed beta, sitting next to one or two users a day and fixing what he saw. Granola went public only once it beat the notetakers from Zoom and Google. "I would definitely build in private or closed beta until I felt really secure that the product was meaningfully better than the competition." (/episode/U7apPfqHgvg/, at 04:31)
- **Alex Mashrabov** (Founder and CEO, Higgsfield): Ship inside a month and let revenue decide whether you ever need investors. "I would recommend focusing on bringing the first dollar by day 30 of product development and maybe $1 million ARR by day 90" (/episode/czItVEAINqw/, at 18:36)
- **Young Zhao** (Co-founder and CEO, OpusClip): Two or three weeks understanding one customer profile, then a prototype in Cursor, then back to those customers to ask what they would pay. "The step two is actually just a couple of days. That should be good enough to have a proof of concept." (/episode/HQ3eVt2jgAY/, at 02:51) Before OpusClip had a product, his team hand-made clips and emailed them out; over 60% of replies were positive.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need to know how to code?

Less than you think. Amjad Masad (/episode/3FgPLbfs_oY/) runs Replit at about $160M ARR with around 350,000 active paid apps, and his advice is to think like an entrepreneur and iterate with the agent instead of debugging line by line. Mike Krieger (/episode/Q66gsBJnKKQ/) says non-engineers at Anthropic ship internal tools by working with Claude on weekends. Anton Osika (/episode/KEAYPqQG2-M/) still names the point where you need a developer, so watch that chapter before you commit.

### What kind of business should I start?

The founders keep pointing at unglamorous industries. In the boring-businesses compilation (/episode/cVGHg4Vd9uM/), Alex Mashrabov uses property management, where money leaks between the listing, the viewing and the paperwork. Young Zhao (/episode/ZK-TCkETAFw/) says segment until you cannot subdivide further. Mati Staniszewski (/episode/EUrkIpq838c/) suggests setting up voice agents for local businesses like doctors' offices as a $10K a month service. Daniel Priestley (/episode/tSrN8eRMEAE/) built a $100K a month business on GPT wrappers.

### Should I raise money?

Alex Mashrabov (/episode/czItVEAINqw/) says many AI businesses can reach tens of millions in revenue profitably, so decide about VC after day 90, and he is not sure everyone needs a Series A. Grant Lee (/episode/LAEaSbZ2IR8/) raised in two weeks of back-to-back Zoom pitches with under a 50% hit rate, and says the biggest fundraising mistake is not planning distribution from day one.

### How fast can this go?

Higgsfield reached $200M ARR in 9 months (/episode/czItVEAINqw/). Aravind Srinivas (/episode/U7PcyE0p54s/) took Perplexity from about $150M to $20B in roughly two years on 1% daily improvement, which compounds to about 3,700% a year. I use the same 1% rule in my solo $1M business episode (/episode/dM5T0Oj9-90/). Replit is growing about 25% month over month (/episode/3FgPLbfs_oY/).

### What should I do in the first 30 days?

Alex Mashrabov (/episode/czItVEAINqw/) wants monetisation in place by day 30, with first customers from organic social and creator integrations. Young Zhao (/episode/HQ3eVt2jgAY/) spends the first two to three weeks on one customer profile before building anything. In the six-ideas compilation (/episode/gwsaC3WiCqs/), the AI-consultant route is 30 days of posting case studies with screenshots and metrics on LinkedIn until the first client arrives.
