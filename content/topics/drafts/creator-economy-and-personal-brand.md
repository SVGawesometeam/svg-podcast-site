## Marina's take

How do I build an audience and make money from it? I have 17 million followers across platforms and I still do not get 1 million views per video. Algorithms now serve content by behaviour.

Get found. On social, packaging is 80% of the result: the gap between 2 million and 28 million views on one of my videos was the title and thumbnail. Robby Stein at Google Search says AI recommends businesses the way a person would, from third-party mentions and press. We rebuilt this podcast's site with full plain-text transcripts and went from invisible to 5th most visible of 11 tracked podcast brands in two months.

Own the audience. Email drives 21 to 27% of revenue across my companies and beats both ads and YouTube. Instagram brought 400 subscribers in 30 days at a 56% open rate; YouTube subscribers open at 87%.

Sell something of your own. Cassey Ho stopped doing brand deals; over 99% of her revenue is apparel, and about 70% of it comes from organic videos. Daniel Priestley gives new personal brands two to three years before AI-amplified incumbents crowd every platform. Trust is the asset: Slow Ventures raised $60 million to back creators who have it, and led my $1.7 million round.

You do not need a big audience to start. UGC is paid for the content itself, and a creator with 700 followers can trade one post for $300 in products from a local business.

## Questions people ask

### Do I need a lot of followers to make money?

No. In the zero-followers episode (/episode/VN_0zJXBO6E/), creators serving entrepreneurs charge about 5x more per sponsored video than entertainment creators with bigger audiences, brands are moving to micro-influencers under 10,000 followers, and UGC is paid for the content itself. In the viral guide (/episode/jVD8pCZeLww/) I explain why follower counts stopped predicting views.

### Which income stream should I start with?

Depends on what you can make. Cassey Ho (/episode/4jqrK-hUicQ/) moved almost entirely to product. Affiliate marketing (/episode/VHiyfK06n1c/) pays per sale, per lead at $5 to $10 for a signup, or up to $300 for a converted high-ticket lead, and keeps paying for years. My merch store (/episode/ZYT81iUfBK4/) went live in about 7 minutes with a 50-hoodie run. My 19 income streams at 34 are listed in (/episode/d_Dp1vpDCrk/).

### How do I get AI assistants to recommend me?

Make your site readable to machines and earn mentions elsewhere. In the GEO guide (/episode/2NFQ4-6MzuU/), static HTML with full transcripts added 8,000 to 15,000 words per episode that AI can read, and ChatGPT recommendations converted at 15.9% against 1.76% from organic Google. Robby Stein (/episode/NPiKEwTSwn4/) says a single AI-recommended mention has doubled a restaurant's bookings overnight. 92% of marketers plan to do this; 40% are doing it.

### How do I keep up with every platform?

Repurpose instead of recreating. I publish 50-plus posts a week using Poppy AI (/episode/jVD8pCZeLww/). Peter Yang (/episode/Yu0z7-KMHpo/) posts to X, LinkedIn, Threads and Substack from one voice note. Our Shorts agent (/episode/1gm__VUG2m8/) publishes up to 10 a day for about $40 a month in tools.

### Is it too late to start a personal brand?

Not yet, and the window is measured. In the seven-founders episode (/episode/rxR83MF6LF0/), Daniel Priestley gives two to three years, and 2,000 to 20,000 people who trust you is enough to build on. Young Zhao (/episode/HQ3eVt2jgAY/) expects competition among creators to get harder and points to creativity and differentiation. My 30-day framework (/episode/KfoenLbhOTI/) starts with ChatGPT finding your niche from what it already knows about you.
