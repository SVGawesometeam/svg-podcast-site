## Marina's take

Which AI tools and agent workflows are worth setting up, and how? My team tested more than 100 tools in a year and kept 11. The guests who get the most from AI run it as a system. Only 15% of CEOs report real value from AI, and the ones who do spend at least 8 hours a week building the skill.

The order matters. Thibault Sottiaux at OpenAI says start with three files: examples of your tone of voice, project notes and contacts. Allie Miller writes three context documents before any agent. We did the same at my company with skills files in Claude Projects for brand guidelines, recruiting and decision frameworks, and our monthly content output and revenue doubled.

Then one agent. Howie Liu's first step is a repetitive task with human review in the loop. Peter Yang's posting system takes one voice note and publishes to X, LinkedIn, Threads and Substack. Allie runs 36 workflows and about 100 agents, and puts the gain at 2x to 10x depending on the task. Our Shorts agent, built in Nathan with Clap AI and ChatGPT, publishes up to 10 Shorts a day for about $40 a month in tools.

Use voice. Level 1 of my self-running company setup is switching from typing to dictation. Greg Brockman no longer writes code himself; he directs the AI and gives feedback.

Conor Grennan counts a power user at about 30 uses a day. Most of us are far below that.

## Where the guests disagree

How much of the work should you still do by hand?

- **Conor Grennan** (Chief AI Architect, NYU Stern): Write the draft yourself, then paste it in and ask how to make it more readable and what you are missing. Let AI start and you cannot get the AI out of it. "It's write your first draft first. Always, always, always." (/episode/_aUKWYHLKfE/, at 06:27)
- **Thibault Sottiaux** (Head of ChatGPT and Codex, OpenAI): Do not write a description of yourself at all. Give the agent past newsletters, recordings and messages to friends, and let it work out your voice. "I don't recommend actually trying to explain your tone of voice." (/episode/DPe_srf0GlI/, at 05:54)

## Questions people ask

### Which tools should I pay for?

Fewer than you think. In my 100-tools test (/episode/8W5A7fIuA-A/), ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Perplexity all work on free tiers until you hit a usage ceiling, and the gain comes from connecting the tools you already have. The compilation on top-1% workflows (/episode/Y854KRfONAU/) is the counterweight: Khan Academy's AI bill runs about $1.2 million a year, because at scale the spend is the point. In my self-running company episode (/episode/Baa71rPgxvA/) I treat high API credit usage as a sign of a lean team.

### How do I build my first agent without code?

Howie Liu (/episode/bzfklNb1wjc/) gives three steps: pick a repetitive task you handle, deploy an agent with human review, then loosen the review as the feedback loop improves. Allie Miller (/episode/YfRkj9kmQf0/) schedules agents in Claude Co-work, Codex and Claude Code entirely in plain language. Our YouTube Shorts agent (/episode/1gm__VUG2m8/) connects Google Sheets, Clap AI and ChatGPT through Nathan and needed no code.

### What is a context file, and do I need one?

Yes, before anything else. Thibault Sottiaux (/episode/DPe_srf0GlI/) names three: tone-of-voice examples, project notes and contacts. Allie Miller (/episode/YfRkj9kmQf0/) builds three context documents first. Peter Yang (/episode/Yu0z7-KMHpo/) keeps his strategy in a Google Doc so his AI advisor gives specific advice. In the income stack compilation (/episode/zL2PIa72gJ4/) I walk through the skills files we standardised across the company.

### How do I get better answers out of the same model?

Give it more. Young Zhao (/episode/zL2PIa72gJ4/) treats ChatGPT as a thinking partner over 20-plus rounds with full context. Mo Gawdat, in the same episode, runs Gemini, DeepSeek and ChatGPT against each other to check answers. Conor Grennan (/episode/_aUKWYHLKfE/) uses "push back" and "what am I missing" prompts. Erik Brynjolfsson (/episode/Y854KRfONAU/) asks Claude or ChatGPT to interview him about his job and generate 10 use cases.

### How much should I trust an agent to run alone?

Keep a human at the end. Peter Yang (/episode/Yu0z7-KMHpo/) reserves the last 10% of any output for his own edit. Howie Liu (/episode/bzfklNb1wjc/) says the leverage comes from humans giving judgment to many agents, like a CEO managing people. Thibault Sottiaux (/episode/DPe_srf0GlI/) describes auto review, where a second agent checks the first before anything with personal data goes out.
