**RESEARCH**

**WHO HE IS**

Brian Chesky is co-founder and CEO of Airbnb, a marketplace where people rent out homes, and now also experiences, services and hotel rooms, to travellers. Airbnb takes a cut of each booking, so its revenue tracks booking volume rather than owning any property. He is 45 (born 29 August 1981) and the only Fortune 500 CEO who went to design school — Rhode Island School of Design, industrial design — a fact he states plainly: "the objective stat is I believe I'm the only person that went to design school in the Fortune 500" ([17:01](https://youtu.be/oC2KMh8zvDw&t=1021s), VERBATIM). His current hook is double: Airbnb just posted its strongest quarter in years, and in June 2026 Bloomberg reported he is personally funding a separate AI lab outside Airbnb — his first move into the AI race ([Bloomberg via Fortune, 4 Jun 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/04/airbnb-ceo-brian-chesky-plans-to-start-a-new-ai-company/); [TechCrunch, 4 Jun 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/04/airbnbs-brian-chesky-plans-to-launch-a-new-ai-lab/)). He confirmed the lab exists on 10 August 2026 and refused to say more: "I've never actually publicly even confirmed that this exists. I can confirm it exists, but I'm going to not say anything more about it right now" ([Yahoo Finance / Power Players with Brian Sozzi](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/airbnb-ceo-brian-chesky-on-his-ai-lab-building-a-super-app-and-how-travel-will-change-forever-134708028.html), VERBATIM). He speaks at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia conference **today, 8 September 2026, 3:05pm PT** ([Airbnb IR](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260825sf31055/airbnb-to-participate-in-the-goldman-sachs-communacopia-technology-conference-2026)).

**PATH**

He grew up in upstate New York, hit puberty two to three years after his peers, and was taken to endocrinologists because his parents thought he had a growth hormone deficiency ([19:56](https://youtu.be/oC2KMh8zvDw&t=1196s)). In 2007 he was a designer in San Francisco with $1,000 in the bank when his roommate Joe Gebbia said the rent had gone up. A design conference was in town and the hotels were sold out. They inflated three air beds and put up airbedandbreakfast.com; three guests stayed, they made rent, and Chesky's line is that he did it for fun, not money: "if I was trying to make money, I would have come up with a better idea than air bed and breakfast" ([43:50](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2630s), VERBATIM). ([1:19:51](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=4791s), VERBATIM). Airbnb IPO'd in December 2020 at roughly a $100B valuation, seven months after cutting 1,900 jobs — 25% of staff — as travel collapsed ([CNBC, 5 May 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/05/airbnb-to-lay-off-nearly-1900-people-25percent-of-company.html)).

2. **He says a one-person billion-dollar company is now plausible — and that he codes.** "It's never been easier to start a company. And I do think a one-person company could be a billion-dollar company." And: "I'd never consider myself a technical founder. Today I'd be coding. In fact, I am coding in Claude Code." 3. **A ten-person team inside Airbnb generated $200M in year one.** "Project Hawaii" was a squad of 10–12 designers, engineers, data scientists and PMs pointed at one metric — search-to-book conversion. Year one it delivered the equivalent of ~$200M in internal revenue, year two $400–500M, and now runs at over 600 basis points on roughly $134B of volume ([23:09](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1389s)).

4. **His scaling rule is one city, then ten, then everywhere.** Services and experiences launched in 100 cities and flopped. He restarted with experiences in Paris and services in LA. "Airbnb launched in New York, Uber launched in San Francisco, DoorDash launched like Palo Alto. Let's make the problem small and just perfect a city" ([25:57](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1557s), VERBATIM). Rule: "Make the problem as small as possible. Dominate a niche" ([26:43](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1603s)). Peter Thiel, an early investor, told him it's better to have a monopoly of a tiny market than a small share of a big one ([26:50](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1610s)).

5. **The highest-rated Airbnbs belong to people with exactly one listing.** "We found the highest rated Airbnbs are people that manage one listing. The second highest are people who manage two. The third highest are people who manage three. You see a pattern." ([3:51](https://youtu.be/TG7zfVfnzW4&t=231s), VERBATIM). He said this in direct answer to a question about the "lease ten properties and Airbnb them" courses sold online.

6. **Creators are monetising offline through Airbnb Experiences.** He says influencers tell him experiences let them monetise a skill and actually meet the people who follow them: "There's an abstraction to posting and just getting likes and comments. **HIS THESIS / WHAT HE SAYS PUBLICLY**

Three arguments run through everything he said in the last twelve months.

**AI is a tailwind for anyone touching the real world, and a threat to pure software layers.** "From a business standpoint, I think AI is the best thing that ever happened to Airbnb" ([2:33](https://youtu.be/TwiVY3M_W0E&t=153s), VERBATIM). His reasoning: chatbot traffic converts better than search traffic, and most of Airbnb's work — host tooling, payments, support, five million hosts — is operational, not a thin software layer AI can eat. He expects the word itself to disappear: "you could replace the term AI with electricity… intelligence will be ubiquitous" ([17:37](https://youtu.be/XmYDXO71rCA&t=1057s), VERBATIM).

**AI turns consumers into creators.** "I think AI shifts our attention from consumption to creation… suddenly all of us have a paintbrush and a canvas to make stuff" ([41:20](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2480s), VERBATIM). He argues most people wrongly believe they aren't creative — "you ask the average person, about one in two says they're not… it's just that they haven't exercised the muscle" ([42:47](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2567s)) — and that the constraint was never imagination but craft. He also separates two meanings of "creator": "creator has been performance. Now creator can be create. Make." ([1:17:14](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=4634s), VERBATIM).

**Founder mode is now a survival requirement, not a style.** Paul Graham coined the term in a September 2024 essay based on a Chesky talk ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founder_mode)). Chesky's version: founders over-delegate to professional managers, stop auditing the work, and lose the steering wheel. His fix is inverted — "you actually want to start hands-on under control and give ground grudgingly. Everyone does the opposite" ([10:54](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=654s), VERBATIM). He is explicit about who he thinks doesn't make it: "The two types of people that will not survive the age of AI are pure people managers who think it's all about just leadership… and people that are rigid and don't want to change" ([14:04](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=844s), VERBATIM). He says he does not think people managers will have any value in future, and that everyone will need to be a hybrid manager-IC — including lawyers, who "have to actually read the case law" ([13:31](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=811s)).

He is also willing to say his own experience is now a liability: "I'm 44. I'm not old, but I'm not young. I'm not AI native in the same way a 22-year-old is" ([59:13](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=3553s), VERBATIM). And he expects apps to end: "I don't think there will be apps in the future, I think there'll be agents" ([1:03:21](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=3801s), VERBATIM).

**THE 11-STAR EXERCISE — WORTH DOING ON CAMERA**

His most transferable framework. Because everyone leaves five stars, five stars means nothing went wrong, not that anything was good. So he writes out the ladder above it. Six stars: your favourite wine, fruit and a handwritten card waiting. Seven: a car at the airport and a surfboard because they know you surf. Eight: an elephant at the airport and a parade in your honour. Nine: the Beatles check-in, 5,000 teenagers screaming your name. Ten: Elon Musk greets you and takes you to space ([38:32](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2312s)). The point is not the ten. "You keep pushing to go so absurd to 10 stars that suddenly six or seven stars doesn't seem crazy at all… the difference between the five and six star is probably the difference between you and a competitor" ([39:31](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2371s), VERBATIM). This works live: pick a viewer's business and walk it up the ladder.

**QUICK EXPLAINER — WHY THE AIRBNB MODEL MATTERS HERE**

Airbnb doesn't own homes. It matches supply and demand and takes a fee, so its costs barely rise when volume does — which is why it runs a ~35% adjusted EBITDA margin on $3.6B of quarterly revenue. That structure is why Chesky's advice generalises to a viewer building anything: the leverage is in getting one small market to work perfectly, then repeating the pattern, not in owning assets.

**WHY THE SHIFT IS HAPPENING NOW**

Three things converged. The tooling collapsed the distance between an idea and a working thing — Chesky's own number is idea-to-launch up to 60% faster and ~80% more features shipped year over year at a company of 8,200 people. Second, capital and talent piled into enterprise AI, leaving consumer almost empty: 159 of 175 companies in the last YC batch. Third, distribution stayed open — the top three apps in the App Store are AI, which he cites as proof that something genuinely new still finds its way to the top ([18:19](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1099s)). His conclusion is that the crowded side is enterprise and the open side is consumer.

**THE NUMBERS**

Airbnb: $3.6B revenue in Q2 2026 (+17%), $27.2B gross bookings (+16%), $816M net income, 148.3M nights and seats booked, ~8,200 employees, roughly $134B in annual volume, and by Chesky's own framing "for every thousand dollars spent in the world, $1 was spent on an Airbnb" ([25:00](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1500s)). **DEFENDED POSITIONS & PUSHBACK FILE**

**1. "Founder mode is the only way to operate in the age of AI"** ([32:07](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=1927s)). **Counter:** the dominant criticism since Graham's 2024 essay is that founder mode is micromanagement with better branding — that founders who won't let go create dependency, stall decisions and lose the people who want autonomy ([UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/apple-with-worms-why-founder-mode-is-a-rebranding-for-micromanaging-top-down-leaders-eb23d89e6c89); [Oxide Computer](https://oxide.computer/blog/reflections-on-founder-mode)). The sharpest version is sampling bias: we only hear from the founders whose micromanagement coincided with success. **His documented defence:** he concedes the first attempt caused "giant revolts" and that the people who hated it left ([36:20](https://youtu.be/eURcW5_uS60&t=2180s)), and he reframes it as sequencing rather than permanence — hands-on first, then "give ground grudgingly," the phrase he credits to Ben Horowitz ([31:46](https://youtu.be/oC2KMh8zvDw&t=1906s)). He also cites Jony Ive telling him Steve Jobs never felt like a micromanager "cuz Steve was partnering with me" ([30:52](https://youtu.be/oC2KMh8zvDw&t=1852s)).

**2. "Banning Airbnb doesn't lower rents."** On CNBC in May he made the New York case directly: two years after the ban, "housing prices did not go down. They went up 8%," and hotels went up more; and Airbnb's top host occupations are retirees, healthcare workers, teachers and construction workers ([5:13](https://youtu.be/TG7zfVfnzW4&t=313s), [5:41](https://youtu.be/TG7zfVfnzW4&t=341s), VERBATIM). **Counter:** New York's Local Law 18 was designed to remove illegal whole-unit listings, and it did — Airbnb listings in NYC fell from roughly 22,000 to about 3,200, and the city's own review credits it with eliminating tens of thousands of illegal rentals ([NYC Mayor's Office of Criminal Justice](https://criminaljustice.cityofnewyork.us/press-release/ll18-report-sheds-light-on-eliminated-illegal-rentals-in-nyc/); [Local Law 18 overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Law_18_of_2022)). Rents rising afterwards doesn't isolate the law's effect, and reform bills stalled without a vote in December 2025 ([Skift](https://skift.com/2025/12/18/no-vote-on-nyc-bill-to-ease-short-term-rental-rules/)). Marina should keep this on the mechanics of the platform, not on any named official.

**3. "Hosts are entrepreneurs, so we can't over-control them"** ([2:26](https://youtu.be/TG7zfVfnzW4&t=146s)). **Counter:** ahead of the 2026 World Cup, hosts have been cancelling confirmed bookings to relist at multiples of the original price, and the arbitrage is large enough that Airbnb's cancellation penalty doesn't deter it — one documented Kansas City case showed a ~719% return even after the penalty ([AirROI analysis](https://www.airroi.com/blog/world-cup-2026-cancellation-arbitrage-airbnb)). Separately, hosts and guests publicly complained that his May 2026 feature announcements ignored their actual issues, chiefly customer support and safety ([The Travel](https://www.thetravel.com/new-airbnb-features-infuriate-travelers-and-hosts-as-bigger-issues-remain-overlooked/)). **His documented defence:** he says Airbnb is "really, really trying to crack down on this," cites blocking price gouging during the LA fires as precedent, and frames it as a deliberate trade-off between marketplace health and host independence ([2:03](https://youtu.be/TG7zfVfnzW4&t=123s)).
