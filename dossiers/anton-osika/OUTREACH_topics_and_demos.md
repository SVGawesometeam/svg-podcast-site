### Interview proposal — topics, demo formats, and why this audience

## THE SHORT VERSION

Anton built Lovable for the 99% who can't code. Almost every interview he gives is to the 1%: Sequoia, All-In, This Week in Startups, HumanX. Founders, investors, operators.

Our audience is the 99% he actually built it for — and, by his own data, the exact person already paying for it.

On 7 September he posted that **55% of Lovable users have 11 or more years of professional experience**. That is our viewer, almost precisely: 25–45, deep expertise in a real domain, employed or side-hustling, no company of their own, actively looking for the thing to build. They are not in the tech bubble. They are the ones who don't yet know Lovable exists.

We'd like to make the episode that reaches them.

## WHY THIS ONE IS DIFFERENT

We watched all five of Anton's major interviews from the last twelve months before writing this. We know what he's already answered, and we don't intend to ask it again.

**We'll skip:** the bike ride to Fabian's place, "0.5% of the world can code," the Q Group $3M in 48 hours, "are we in a bubble," and why Stockholm. He's told each of these at least three times on camera.

**We'll spend the time on** what nobody has asked him: who the 55% actually are, what they should build this year, and what the first thirty days look like for someone who has never shipped anything.

## TOPICS

**1. Who is actually building — the 55%**
We want Anton to describe that person: what they build first, what they get wrong, and what separates the ones who turn it into income from the ones who stop after one weekend.

**2. What to build right now**
He sees roughly 200,000 new projects a day. Which categories are working, which are already crowded, and what he'd personally build today with no company and no team.

**3. The first thirty days, concretely**
His own advice is to sit down for one full day with a new tool and you'll be ahead of almost everyone in your company. We want to make that specific and non-negotiable: day one, week one, first paying customer.

**4. The builder stories, with the part everyone skips**
The Swedish dog-portrait business at $300K a month, Klar's €130K ARR in 30 days, the 80,000-agent replatform. Our audience needs the second half of each story: distribution, pricing, how the first customers were found. Building is the easy part now, and Anton has said so.

**5. Past vibes — what the work requires now**
He wrote in July that he's done saying "vibe coding." We'd like to give that its own segment: what changed, what building properly means today, and where the real skill sits once the code stops being the hard part. Natural home for the security-by-default and AIUC-1 work.

**6. Product judgment as the new bottleneck**
His argument that the constraint is empathy and product taste, not engineering — and his more recent line that creativity is becoming the only moat. Our audience wants to know whether a non-technical person can build that judgment, and how.

**7. Building from anywhere**
Lovable is Stockholm, not San Francisco. Most of our audience isn't in a tech hub either. What he'd say to someone in Warsaw, São Paulo or Lisbon who assumes they have to move first.

## DEMO IDEAS

Three options. Any one stands alone; we'd lock the choice with your team in advance so nothing is improvised on the day.

### A. Build a real viewer's business, live *(our first choice)*
We collect submissions from our audience before filming and pick one real person with a real problem and no technical background. Anton and Marina build it on camera from a blank prompt, and Anton narrates the decisions as he makes them — what to cut, what to name, what to charge. We follow up with that person 30 days later for a short second video.

*Why it works:* the product's entire promise, proven in real time, on a real stranger rather than a demo account. The viewer watches someone like themselves go from nothing to a working product inside one segment. It also produces exactly the kind of story Lovable already publishes weekly, except this one is filmed.
*Needs:* ~15 minutes on camera, screen share, one account.

### B. Rebuild something we currently pay for
We take a SaaS tool our own team subscribes to and Anton rebuilds our version of it live. Real numbers on screen: what we pay a year, how long the rebuild took, what it costs to run.

*Why it works:* it's his strongest existing case study — the $500,000 intranet quote that took four hours — but happening to us, on camera, against our actual invoice. Very hard to argue with, and it lands with the large share of our audience who work inside companies rather than founding them.
*Needs:* ~10–12 minutes. We'd send the tool and requirements ahead so Anton isn't going in cold.

### C. The one-day challenge, graded
Marina spends a week building on Lovable beforehand, with no help. She brings what she made. Anton reviews it live — what's good, what's broken, what she'd never have known to ask for — and fixes it on camera.

*Why it works:* it shows the honest learning curve instead of a polished demo, which is what makes it credible to a skeptical viewer. It also tests his own claim about what one focused day gets you, with a host who is not an engineer.
*Needs:* ~15 minutes, plus build access a week ahead.
