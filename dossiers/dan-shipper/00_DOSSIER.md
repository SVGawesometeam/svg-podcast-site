## WHO HE IS

Dan Shipper is the co-founder and CEO of [Every](https://every.to/), a ~30-person, Brooklyn-based media-and-software company that has become the most-cited real-world test case in the "will AI take jobs" debate. Every sells one subscription ($30/month or $288/year — [every.to/subscribe](https://every.to/subscribe)) that bundles AI journalism (his column Chain of Thought, read by 130,000+ people — [danshipper.com](https://danshipper.com/)) with a suite of AI apps — Cora (email), Spiral (writing), Sparkle (file organization), Monologue (dictation), Proof (collaborative editor), Plus One (Slack agent) — plus a consulting arm for executives ([Platformer, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.platformer.news/every-dan-shipper-interview-ai-writing/)). He hosts the podcast AI & I and has been a Sequoia scout since January 2023 ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/danshipper/)). The current hook: his May 21, 2026 essay ["After Automation"](https://every.to/p/after-automation) — AI creates more human work, not less, and Every doubled headcount while automating everything as proof — went viral (reactions from Marc Andreessen and Lenny Rachitsky — [X, 27 May 2026](https://x.com/danshipper/status/2059673326247625084)), and Every's first conference, Thesis, happens November 5, 2026 at Pioneer Works in Brooklyn ([announcement, 13 Aug 2026](https://every.to/on-every/introducing-thesis-2027)).

## PATH

Philosophy degree at Penn, 2010–2014. As a student he co-founded Firefly, a co-browsing/customer-support tool, with Justin Meltzer — bootstrapped except $20K from First Round's Dorm Room Fund — and sold it to Pegasystems in June 2014 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2014/06/09/pegasystems-acquires-co-browsing-tool-firefly-the-first-investment-by-first-rounds-dorm-room-fund)). VERBATIM: "I flew from my college graduation to Boston to finish negotiating the deal to sell it" ([Big Think, 21 Nov 2025, [23:16]](https://youtu.be/jkXOudImoI0)). He then wrote the Superorganizers productivity newsletter, and co-founded Every in 2020 with Nathan Baschez, merging their two newsletters into a paid bundle over a Thanksgiving phone call ([Mercury profile](https://mercury.com/blog/what-comes-next-dan-shipper-every)). Baschez left about 2–3 years in to run the AI word processor Lex; Dan says the company "almost fell apart" ([Casey Newton, 20 Aug 2026, [22:46]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)). He was diagnosed with OCD in his late 20s and talks about it openly: "exposure and response prevention and Zoloft — incredible combination, highly recommend… I would not be able to run this company without that" (VERBATIM, [same, [22:20–22:29]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)).

## HIS THESIS / WHAT HE SAYS PUBLICLY

His central framework is the **allocation economy**: "we're moving from a knowledge economy to an allocation economy. In a knowledge economy, you are compensated based on what you know. In an allocation economy, you're compensated based on how well you allocate the resources of intelligence" — manager skills, currently held by ~7% of the economy, become everyone's job (VERBATIM, [Big Think, [51:46]](https://youtu.be/jkXOudImoI0)). The After Automation corollary: "Automation is a lie. Every agent needs a human" — every automated system needs a human steward, so automation creates work ([Lenny, [0:24], [39:14]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)). On jobs: "the AI job apocalypse is not really a thing" — "what models do is make yesterday's human competence cheap," and humans stay valuable by recombining it into new things ([Lenny, [1:13:11], [1:13:52]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)). His advice to individuals is concrete: "the only thing you need to do is ride the models" ([[1:16:29]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)), and "the edge of AI is wherever AI meets a real human doing something," not San Francisco ([[1:18:44]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)) — Every itself is in Brooklyn. On AI writing: slop is content where "it took them less time to make it than it takes me to read it, and they don't stand behind every line" ([[1:04:51]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)); authorship = ownership, not keystrokes: "whether or not you type the words does not really matter to me, but they have to be yours" ([Casey Newton, [53:41]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)).

## QUICK EXPLAINER (readable on air)

Every is a new species of company: a magazine that builds software. One ~$20/month subscription gets you their AI journalism plus six AI apps — email, writing, dictation, file cleanup. The twist is how it's made: about 30 people, each product run by roughly one person, and 99% of the code written by AI agents while humans direct them. It's the closest thing to a live experiment in what work looks like after automation — which is exactly what Dan writes about.

## WHY IT'S HAPPENING NOW

Coding agents crossed a threshold in 2025: one person can now build and maintain a production product ([AI Engineer, [16:30]](https://youtu.be/MGzymaYBiss)). Every reorganized around that fact before almost anyone, so when the jobs debate exploded in 2026 — Stanford data showing AI-exposed young workers 19% less employed ([cited in the Casey Newton episode, [0:36–11:05]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)) — Every became the counter-example both sides argue over: the company that automated everything and doubled hiring. After Automation (May 21) drove the discourse; Thesis (Nov 5) is the attempt to own the question institutionally.

## THE SIZE / THE NUMBERS

Deliberately small — that's the story. ~$1M actually drawn of a $2M seed, ~30 people, six products, 7,000+ paying / 100,000+ free subscribers (Dec 2025), consulting ~$1M/year (mid-2025). Compare: he says the New York Times needed "150 years and a lot of scale" to build its games-cooking-Athletic bundle; Every is doing the bundle model "much more quickly with less money" ([Casey Newton, [40:40]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)). For the viewer, the frame is replicability: this is what one AI-pilled person per product now produces.

## DEFENDED POSITIONS & PUSHBACK FILE

**1. "No AI job apocalypse — automation creates human work."** Strongest counter: the Stanford "canaries" data — young workers in AI-exposed jobs now 19% less employed, up from 15% a year earlier — presented in the same episode where he appears ([Casey Newton episode intro](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)); plus Lenny's flat-designer-hiring data, which he conceded was "really interesting. We'll see" ([Lenny, [1:12:44]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)). His defense: displacement is real but the mechanism is over-hiring corrections wearing an AI excuse ([[1:13:24]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)), and cheap competence floods fields with almost-right output that experts must finish ([After Automation](https://every.to/p/after-automation)).

**2. "AI writing is legitimate if you stand behind it."** Counter: on June 8, 2026 he posted "this is good" about an AI-assisted article and replies called it "so much AI slop in there, it's hard to read" ([X, 8 Jun 2026](https://x.com/danshipper/status/2063948403566854585); backlash covered by [The Fix, 27 Aug 2026](https://thefix.media/2026/08/27/are-we-seeing-backlash-to-ai-slop/)); Casey Newton pressed on authorship and disclosure and Dan's answer stayed unresolved ([[53:50]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)). His defense: the slop definition (time + standing behind every line), the typewriter-era norms analogy, and "I'm in the driver's seat" ([[1:00:22]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)).

**3. "Codex surpassed Claude" (and Every as neutral model arbiter).** Counter: the neutrality question — he pre-rebuts it himself: "People are like, 'Oh, are you sponsored by OpenAI?' And I'm like, 'No, I just talk about what I like'" ([Lenny, [1:32:30]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)). His defense: Every publishes negative reviews of every lab (Casey cites their critical Sonnet 5 review, [30:58](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)), labs "welcome the real feedback" even when relations "get a little bit heated" ([[31:58], [32:38]](https://youtu.be/s0fwOVf2WTw)), and he hedges the call as "a horse race… I think it'll change" ([Lenny, [20:58]](https://youtu.be/4D3hDmGhFhA)).

No lawsuits, regulatory actions, or personal scandals found in the news sweep — the criticism trail is entirely the AI-slop/disclosure debate above.
