Fei-Fei Li built ImageNet, the foundational dataset that launched the deep learning revolution, and now runs World Labs, where she recently raised $1 billion to advance AI's understanding of physical space. David Rogier is the CEO of Masterclass, the online learning platform valued at $2.75 billion, and is known for building his own AI-powered productivity tools rather than relying on commercial software.

In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Fei-Fei Li and David Rogier, Fei-Fei Li: Founder of World Labs · David Rogier: CEO of Masterclass. In this episode, Marina Mogilko interviews Fei-Fei Li, who built ImageNet—the dataset that sparked the deep learning revolution—and David Rogier, CEO of Masterclass (valued at $2.75 billion), about the future of work in an AI-driven world. Rather than endorsing either extreme (AI will save us all or destroy all jobs), the guests present a nuanced view: AI is a powerful tool that widens the gap between those who use it effectively and those who don't. David shares how he's shifted from buying software to building custom AI apps in a weekend, including a to-do list that auto-deletes tasks sitting longer than 1.5 days. Fei-Fei discusses her new $1 billion initiative at World Labs focused on teaching AI to understand physical space—spatial intelligence—something the technology still struggles with. The conversation reveals that the real divide isn't between employed and unemployed, but between high-agency workers who embrace AI and those hesitant to engage with it.

Key Takeaways

  • In 10 years, there will be two kinds of workers: those using AI to multiply their output and those left behind—the gap is widening rapidly as the cost of building custom AI applications has dropped from months to a weekend
  • The polarized public discourse on AI (utopia vs. apocalypse) misses the nuanced middle—AI is a civilizational tool that requires society to learn how to wield it responsibly, much like we learned to use fire, knives, and the internet
  • Agency matters more than tool selection—David Rogier built his entire CEO stack with custom AI apps rather than commercial tools, prioritizing workflows designed for how he actually thinks and works
  • Teaching AI adoption requires personal demonstration, not just resources—David found that walking employees through one basic task step-by-step unlocks their confidence to independently explore AI far more effectively than pointing them to YouTube tutorials
  • Spatial intelligence remains AI's frontier—Fei-Fei Li raised $1 billion for World Labs because current AI excels at pattern recognition in data but still cannot reliably understand physical space and 3D environments
