About the Guest

Daniil and David Liberman
Founders of Gonka · Former founders (sold to Snap) · Investors

Daniil and David Liberman are investors and founders who previously built and sold a company to Snap. They are now building Gonka, a decentralized AI network designed to make artificial intelligence more open and affordable to everyone. The brothers have been in technology circles since at least 2015 and were early believers in Marina Mogilko's vision, leading her first venture round in 2021.

In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Daniil and David Liberman, Founders of Gonka · Former founders (sold to Snap) · Investors. Marina Mogilko sits down with the Liberman brothers to discuss who will control AI access and wealth over the next 3 years. The brothers argue that while AI is often dismissed as overhyped for everyday users, it has already fundamentally changed how people consume information—shifting from multi-source research to trusting single AI sources like ChatGPT. They present a concrete example of how AI solved a 3-year plumbing problem in Marina's house by diagnosing a missing pipe, demonstrating AI's potential to enhance rather than replace skilled labor. The key question they explore is whether AI becomes a freely accessible commodity or remains gated behind Big Tech companies. The brothers predict that within 2 years, several AI breakthroughs—particularly in biotech, genome understanding, and new materials—will create undeniable tipping points where there's no turning back. They emphasize that the window to influence whether the market or a handful of people control AI access is closing, making consumer demand and participation critical right now.

Key Takeaways

  • AI has already changed human behavior — people now trust single AI sources like ChatGPT instead of doing multi-source research, which has real consequences for information consumption and decision-making
  • The plumber example shows AI's true role: enhancing skilled workers' productivity rather than replacing them entirely, but adoption depends on whether workers actively participate in innovation or passively resist it
  • Within 2 years, expect several AI breakthroughs in biotech, genome understanding, and materials science that will be undeniable tipping points — there's a narrow window right now to shape whether these advances benefit everyone or just a few
  • The real wealth and control in AI won't go to the founders who build it — the Anthropic founder owns just 1.5% of his own company — but to whoever controls access and distribution of the technology
  • Consumers still have power right now to demand open AI access and fair rules, but this leverage closes as AI becomes more centralized; every profession will be disrupted on different timelines (software first, then physical industries), giving some groups time to adapt if they actively participate rather than resist
