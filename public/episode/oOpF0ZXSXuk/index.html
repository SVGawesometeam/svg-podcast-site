About the Guest

Dr. Jonathan Chen
Physician, Computer Scientist · Director of AI in Medical Education at Stanford Medicine

Dr. Jonathan Chen is a physician and computer scientist at Stanford Medicine who directs AI in medical education. His randomized controlled trial research examining how doctors use AI for diagnosis has been widely cited and covered by major outlets including the New York Times. He studies how to responsibly integrate AI into clinical practice and medical training.

In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Dr. Jonathan Chen, Physician, Computer Scientist · Director of AI in Medical Education at Stanford Medicine. Dr. Jonathan Chen discusses a counterintuitive finding from his research: GPT-4 alone outperformed doctors who had access to GPT-4 on diagnostic reasoning tasks. While this challenges the assumption that human-AI collaboration always produces better results, Chen explains that the phenomenon stems partly from doctors lacking training in how to use AI effectively—a third of study participants had never used a chatbot before. The real value of doctors lies not in knowledge retrieval, where AI excels, but in understanding context, building patient trust, and making nuanced decisions about care. Chen emphasizes that without proper medical training, people using AI can actually perform worse than without it because they may ask questions incorrectly, leading the AI to confidently guide them down wrong diagnostic paths. He advocates for thoughtful integration of AI into healthcare rather than replacement of physician judgment.

Key Takeaways

  • GPT-4 alone outperformed doctors using GPT-4 on diagnostic tasks, but this doesn't mean doctors are obsolete—it reveals a knowledge retrieval advantage, not a replacement for clinical judgment and patient care
  • How you ask AI health questions matters critically: without medical context and training, people using AI performed worse than without it because they unconsciously guide the AI toward wrong answers
  • AI amplifies whatever approach you take—if you use it well with proper context, it makes good diagnosis better; if you ask it poorly, it confidently confirms your mistakes with high confidence
  • Prepare for doctor appointments by organizing your medical records, lab history, and genetic data in AI, then ask the AI what questions to bring—but present the AI-generated insights as context to explore with your doctor, not as directives
  • The future of doctors isn't being replaced by AI, but being redefined: trust-building, navigating uncertainty, making final judgment calls, and knowing when more screening does more harm than good are increasingly the core of medical value
