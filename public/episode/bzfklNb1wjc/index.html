About the Guest

Howie Liu
Co-founder of Airtable · Building HyperAgent

Howie Liu is the co-founder of Airtable, a no-code platform used by over 80% of Fortune 100 companies. He is now building HyperAgent, a platform for deploying autonomous AI agents. Liu is recognized as a builder and entrepreneur focused on how AI agents will reshape work and company structure.

In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Howie Liu, Co-founder of Airtable · Building HyperAgent. Howie Liu, co-founder of Airtable (used by over 80% of Fortune 100 companies), discusses the evolution of AI from chatbots to autonomous agents. He explains that we've moved beyond the chatbot era into a new phase where models like GPT-5.5 and Claude 2.6 enable agents to operate with near-human autonomy. Liu demonstrates his real productivity setup, including an agent that monitors X 24/7 and surfaces only relevant content, plus agents that executed a full billboard campaign end-to-end. He addresses the misconception that agents can fully run companies autonomously, noting that the most effective deployment still involves human judgment in the loop, but with significantly increased leverage. The key insight is that builders who learn to manage fleets of agents in parallel—similar to how top developers now use multiple AI coding agents simultaneously—will gain superhuman capabilities and competitive advantage.

Key Takeaways

  • AI has reached a step-function change — we're no longer in the chatbot era but in an agent era where models like Claude 2.6 and GPT-5.5 enable near-autonomous execution without constant human correction
  • The most superhuman use case isn't fully autonomous agents running companies, but rather humans who maintain judgment and give feedback to multiple agents working in parallel, creating extreme leverage
  • Building a virtual twin of yourself with AI—an agent trained on your taste and feedback patterns—solves the bottleneck problem where all decisions flow through the founder
  • The two critical skills for the agent era are (1) the tinker mindset—experimentation and comfort with iterating on agent workflows, and (2) the ability to give good judgment and feedback like an effective CEO managing people
  • Start with agents today in three concrete steps: identify a repetitive task you handle, deploy an agent to execute it with human review in the loop, then iterate on the feedback loop to increase autonomy over time
