About the Guest

Sal Khan
Founder and CEO of Khan Academy

Sal Khan is the founder and CEO of Khan Academy, a free online learning platform used by over 200 million people globally. He pioneered early AI tutoring systems and is widely recognized as a thought leader on how AI will reshape education and employment. Khan is currently writing 'Job Shock,' which examines AI's impact on workforce displacement, and is developing a new $10,000 degree program in partnership with TED and ETS centered on skills durable to automation.

In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Sal Khan, Founder and CEO of Khan Academy. Sal Khan, founder of Khan Academy (used by 200 million people worldwide), discusses his new book 'Job Shock' and why he's increasingly concerned about AI's impact on employment. Unlike his previous optimistic stance, Khan now warns that AI could disrupt jobs faster than predicted—citing a real example where a portfolio company automated 80% of a call center workforce in the Philippines, affecting hundreds to thousands of workers. Khan Academy is investing heavily in AI, spending $1.2M annually with Anthropic, and is developing a new version of Khanmigo that's more proactive in motivating students rather than just responsive to requests. Beyond education, Khan is launching a $10,000 accreditation-pending degree with TED and ETS focused on five durable skills that AI cannot replicate, positioning it as an alternative to traditional expensive universities.

Key Takeaways

  • AI job displacement could become visible in 5-10 years, affecting millions in vulnerable sectors like call centers (which represent 5-7% of Philippines GDP) and ride-share driving—faster than most predictions but slower than some AI evangelists claim
  • Khan Academy's $1.2M annual spend with Anthropic signals serious institutional investment in AI; the company is evolving its AI tutor from reactive to proactive to better engage students and keep teachers in the loop
  • Five durable skills that remain AI-resistant are central to Khan's new degree program with TED and ETS, offering a $10K alternative to traditional four-year universities amid higher education disruption
  • AI assessment tools are already being deployed at elite institutions like Caltech for admissions, using oral exams to verify whether students actually wrote their research papers as plagiarism detection evolves
  • Society needs a proactive reskilling strategy—not just optimistic faith that 'the industrial revolution worked out'—because past technological shifts included wars, communism experiments, and significant social disruption
