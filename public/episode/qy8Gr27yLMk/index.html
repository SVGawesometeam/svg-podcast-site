In this episode of the Silicon Valley Girl Podcast, Marina Mogilko interviews Greg Brockman, Co-Founder of OpenAI · Former CTO at Stripe. Greg Brockman, co-founder of OpenAI, discusses why AI adoption remains underutilized despite its capabilities and how proactive AI agents can transform productivity. With 1.2 billion weekly active users on ChatGPT, Brockman notes that reaching just three different use cases within the app converts users into power users who continue engaging. He explains that the gap between AI's potential and its application stems from users struggling to discover what AI can actually do for them—and AI systems themselves need to become more proactive in suggesting capabilities. Brockman demonstrates this through personal examples, including how he no longer writes software himself but directs AI to write code while providing feedback, and shares how AI agents now handle tasks like flagging ignored emails with draft responses. The conversation emphasizes that AI's real value emerges when systems understand context—email, calendar, domain expertise—and can offer unprompted assistance. Brockman predicts a "massive renaissance of entrepreneurship" within the next one to two years, as AI amplifies individual capabilities at scale. He argues that fundamental business principles remain unchanged: building relationships with customers, understanding their needs, and creating genuine value. However, the mechanics of business will evolve, particularly for consumer-facing companies that can now maintain personalized relationships at unprecedented scale using AI-powered relationships and customer care systems.

Key Takeaways

  • OpenAI's 1.2 billion weekly active users reveal a massive adoption gap—reaching just three different use cases converts casual users into power users, indicating most people are barely scratching the surface of what AI can do.
  • Greg Brockman no longer writes code himself; instead he directs AI to write software and provides feedback and guidance, demonstrating the shift from doing work directly to orchestrating AI systems.
  • Proactive AI assistants that understand context (email, calendar, domain expertise) can independently identify opportunities and suggest solutions without being asked—such as flagging ignored emails and drafting responses—representing a fundamental capability shift.
  • Small businesses are easier to start than ever, and the gap is ambition, not access: an antique watch store owner who was sure AI was useless for him ended up with a website and the story behind each watch, built just by talking to the AI.
  • Brockman expects a “massive renaissance of entrepreneurship” over the next one to two years, and a blurrier line between consumer and enterprise: if AI lets you build meaningful relationships at scale, the enterprise sales motion can start to make sense for consumer businesses too.
  • Products that only work around today's model limitations won't last. What stays durable is deep domain expertise and trust with every stakeholder in an industry, so a smarter model makes your product better and more defensible instead of obsolete.
